Deep-Sleep Power Architecture and Hardware Ablation To meet the logistical constraints of a retrofit tap attachment, the HydroTrack v3.0 must operate autonomously on internal battery power for extended durations. This necessitates a fundamental reimagining of the standard microcontroller power paradigm. The default ESP32-S3 DevKitC-1 Mini modules are engineered for rapid desktop prototyping, heavily prioritizing developer convenience over low-power deployment. In deep-sleep mode, an unmodified development board draws an unacceptable 2mA to 10mA of quiescent current, which would drain a standard 400mAh lithium-polymer battery in a matter of days. 51 Achieving the requisite sub-100µA nanopower state requires aggressive physical hardware ablation combined with precise firmware-level isolation. The hardware modification protocol begins with the physical removal of parasitic components on the DevKitC PCB. The onboard CH340 or CP2102N USB-to-UART bridge chip, designed exclusively to interface with a computer during programming, draws continuous leakage current. Furthermore, the ubiquitous red power-indicator LED and its associated current-limiting resistor (often designated R12, R15, or R17 depending on the board revision) must be mechanically severed or desoldered from the PCB. 52 This single LED continuously sinks several milliamperes and provides no functional benefit to the end-user in a deployed IoT scenario. 54 The most significant source of parasitic power loss, however, is the stock AMS1117 or SGM2212 3.3V Low-Dropout (LDO) linear voltage regulator. Standard LDOs possess notoriously high quiescent currents and atrocious dropout efficiencies when stepping down higher battery voltages, bleeding excess energy entirely as heat. To rectify this, the factory LDO is completely desoldered and bypassed. In its place, the architecture integrates the Texas Instruments TPS62840. 56 The TPS62840 is an ultra-high-efficiency step-down (buck) converter specifically engineered for battery-powered IoT applications. It boasts a world-class operating quiescent current ( ) of just 60nA. 56 The TPS62840 utilizes TI's proprietary Direct Control with Seamless Transition into Power Save Mode (DCS-Control) topology. Under heavy computational loads—such as during Wi-Fi negotiation or ESP-NOW data transmission—the converter operates in standard Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) mode to deliver up to 750mA of clean power. 59 As the ESP32-S3 enters deep sleep and the system load drops into the microampere range, the TPS62840 automatically detects the current drop and transitions into Pulse Frequency Modulation (PFM) mode. In PFM, the converter emits a single switching pulse to recharge the output capacitor and subsequently shuts down almost all of its internal circuitry, successfully maintaining the

Cloud Pipeline and Telemetry Routing Architecture The data aggregation and transmission architecture of the HydroTrack ecosystem is meticulously engineered to maintain the absolute dormancy of the battery-powered edge device. Utilizing standard Wi-Fi to transmit telemetry directly to the cloud from the tap attachment would require establishing a full TCP/IP handshake, acquiring a DHCP lease, and negotiating TLS handshakes—a process that takes several seconds and requires hundreds of milliamperes, catastrophically draining the battery. Instead, telemetry data from the HydroTrack v3.0—including precise volumetric flow rates, thermal thresholds from the NTC thermistor, TDS conductivity values, and acoustic anomaly classifications generated by the TinyML CNN—is packed into a highly condensed byte array. This payload is transmitted via the connectionless ESP-NOW protocol. Operating entirely at the MAC layer, ESP-NOW allows the edge tap to wake, broadcast the state update to the local gateway, and return to deep sleep in roughly two to three milliseconds. 6 Upon receiving the MAC-addressed ESP-NOW payload, the continuously powered ESP32 Gateway (plugged into a wall outlet) takes over the heavy computational lifting. The Gateway unpacks the raw byte array and serializes the data into a structured JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) format. 7 A typical JSON payload encapsulates the specific device MAC ID, an NTP-synced timestamp, the kinematic flow parameters, and boolean flags representing any detected leaks, cavitation events, or thermal scalding hazards. The ESP32 Gateway then utilizes its established Wi-Fi connection to publish this JSON payload to a lightweight Message Queuing Telemetry Transport (MQTT) broker, such as HiveMQ. MQTT is the industry standard for IoT telemetry due to its efficient publish-subscribe model, minimal packet overhead, and ability to guarantee message delivery over low-bandwidth, unreliable networks via configurable Quality of Service (QoS) levels. The HiveMQ broker acts as the central, highly scalable data exchange, instantly pushing the MQTT topics to subscribed backend cloud databases (for long-term municipal analysis) and direct to consumer mobile endpoints. The end-user interfaces with this ecosystem via a cross-platform mobile application developed in Flutter. The Flutter app runs an MQTT client in the background, subscribing specifically to the secure topics linked to the user's hardware gateway.
